## The problem

Much published systems and HPC research is hard to reproduce. The experiments depend on specific hardware, configurations and data that other researchers can't easily recreate. Open testbeds like Chameleon can help, but only if researchers know the practices and package their work as reusable artifacts. REPETO set out to build a community that does exactly that.

## My role

I managed the program alongside my work on Chameleon. I coordinated activities, handled NSF reporting, and helped design and run the community initiatives.

## What I did

- **Ran a community workshop on practical reproducibility in HPC.** It produced the report *Challenges of Practical Reproducibility for Systems and HPC Computer Science* (2025), which sets out best practices. I co-authored it as second author.
- **Taught at major conferences.** We ran tutorials for artifact evaluation committees and participants, the people who check whether published results can be reproduced.
- **Engaged students** through reproducibility hackathons.
- **Helped launch a Reproducibility Ambassadors program.** It sponsored students to attend conferences and talk with researchers about reproducibility and how open platforms like Chameleon make it practical.
- **Supported the Summer of Reproducibility,** a mentorship program modeled on Google Summer of Code that another PI led. I helped match students with mentors working on reproducibility challenges in their fields, and co-authored the ACM REP '25 paper on the program.

## Results

By my estimate, the community added about 140 reproducible artifacts to Trovi, Chameleon's artifact repository, during the project, and our events and initiatives reached well over 1,000 researchers. The Summer of Reproducibility mentored 55+ students with 60+ mentors starting in 2023.
